It is neither a renamed CodeBuddy IDE/CLI nor a study limited to voice input. The method cross-checks public official documentation, vendor support pages, named journalists' hands-on reports, original forum posts, and video pages. 1. The Product at a Glance

WorkBuddy is an AI workbench that brings together natural-language tasks, executable tools, local/cloud workspaces, editable deliverables, specialist roles, shared team knowledge, and cross-device entry points. Its core extends beyond “answering questions” or “operating a computer”: it productizes the chain of assign a task → obtain context → plan/call tools → generate or modify files → human review → save, share, hand off, or rerun on a schedule. The home screen covers everyday office work, coding, and creative design; projects, libraries, connectors, experts/expert teams, and skills let work accumulate over time.S02

Research assessment: The competitive unit is the entire “workbench + workflow + Chinese office ecosystem + local/cloud execution” system. A screenshot of a chat box cannot capture it. At the same time, the official promise of being “everywhere” is an ecosystem vision; purchasable hardware, a live integration, and a launch-event demonstration must be treated as three distinct states.

Interaction Is More Than a One-Time Submission

Current task conversations support additional instructions, references to output text/tables/components, process inspection, and a browser on the right. New messages sent during execution enter a queue and can be edited, deleted, or reordered; sending one immediately does not necessarily interrupt the current tool. Deliverables can be inspected in the file tree and changes view, then uploaded to cloud storage, Tencent Docs, or ima/Lexiang. Tasks can be searched, archived, and pinned. Global search across the library and chat history was added in late September.S01, S07, S08

Complete Chains That Explain “Why Use It?”

  1. A journalist writing an article: Newly written Word document → open on the workbench's right side → select an achievements paragraph and convert it to a table → leave revision comments on the relevant paragraph → ask the agent to execute → inspect the original text. The value is reduced shuttling between chat and document, rather than merely generating another answer. Evidence comes from a named journalist's actual operation, but the sample is limited.R01
  2. A non-engineering office team: A manager asks staff to download it → the department deploys internal Skills for requirements, product, and testing → employees use report/PPT tasks. One user finds it too slow; another replies that it is useful for office work. This is evidence of organizational rollout, but the company is unnamed and there is no sustained deployment data.R03
  3. Team project handoff: An administrator configures project standards and tools → member A generates analytical material → hands deliverables, a progress summary, assignee, and deadline to member B → B continues within that context. This is a documented collaboration mechanism, rather than ordinary sharing of a chat link.S19
  4. Plaud meeting follow-through: A recording device captures audio → Plaud transcribes/summarizes and synchronizes to the cloud → an authorized WorkBuddy connector retrieves it → creates a weekly report/customer proposal/follow-up email draft → a person checks before sending. MCP reads existing outputs; it does not start recording or retranscribe audio.H03, H04
  5. A small merchant's lightweight app: Describe course booking, student login, and capacity storage → generate an app with a backend → enable cloud services → preview → publish → manually update the live version after subsequent changes. The official tutorial documents a usable product chain; this study found no independent merchant case with ongoing operating bills or retention data.S12

At Least Three Distinct Integration Routes

  • Computer audio + shortcut peripherals: AU05-X and LARK A2. They reduce the effort of launching, dictating, sending, and switching tasks; execution remains in WorkBuddy. They are not standalone computing/offline agents.
  • Existing vertical app + MCP/embedded agent: Plaud. Hardware captures first, the app transcribes first, and WorkBuddy then handles downstream work. This route most clearly explains “what happens after recording.”
  • Dedicated agent terminals / audiovisual entry points: Anker desktop companion, Rokid glasses, and other launch-event terminals. Their value is on-site input, status, and confirmation, but public evidence for a complete consumer-ready chain is substantially thinner than for the first two categories.

The hardware list is not nine independent user success stories; nine devices also do not mean nine different agent intelligences.

What the Feedback Reasonably Supports

  • Established positive product points: A named article's hands-on work supports in-place co-editing; lightweight forum users in September appreciate simple tasks and lack of configuration; some cross-device users appreciate seeing and replying to computer tasks.R01, R04, R12
  • Specific pain point 1: Mismatched task size. Sending a small edit through a complex agent chain may cost more waiting/explanation than doing it manually. This appears in both July and September accounts and cannot all be attributed to model knowledge.
  • Specific pain point 2: Long-workflow reliability. There are historical interruption reports involving expert teams, custom models, long conversations, and authorization/connection recovery. Corresponding fixes do appear in the official changelog, but a fix announcement does not prove every real-world issue has disappeared.
  • Specific pain point 3: Runtime visibility and startup overhead. A sense that it is “stuck/still processing/returning nothing” affects trust in delivery. This combines UI, runtime, and environment issues. Forum theories involving disk scanning, antivirus, or prompts remain unproven.
  • Evidence still insufficient: Several weeks of business success rates for ordinary finance/HR/sales users, actual costs after payment, post-purchase hardware retention, and permissions/collaboration incidents as teams scale. A remark such as “one day's work reduced to an hour” cannot become a quantitative conclusion.

6. Recent Versions, Costs, and Safety Determine Practical Usability

Version Snapshot

Official 5.7.6 (10/4) fixes failure to create local tasks, PPTX previews, and cloud assistants not replying; 5.7.5 (10/3) adds a combined scheduled-task timeline/deliverable notifications and fixes incomplete long-conversation history and context loss after cancellation; 5.7.0 (9/28) adds global search and workspace trust confirmation; 5.6.0 (9/19) records Browser Use compatibility with Edge/QQ Browser, tool-display improvements, and voice improvements.S01 These establish versions and historical faults, not that those faults still exist or are now 100% resolved.

Version sources also conflict: the 7/30 media report calls V5.3.5 newly launched that day, while the official changelog dates 5.3.5 to 7/25 and 5.3.6 to 7/28. Retain the report's account of the tested version without using it to assert an exact first-release date.R01, S01

Safety and Acceptance of Results

Default permissions request confirmation before sensitive-path access, significant deletions, and high-risk script/network operations. Full access reduces intervention and should not be treated as the recommended default. Skills may run scripts and send data to third parties; project-level shared authorization differs from personal authorization; team-space permissions also constrain the agent. The assistant's independent mailbox retains confirmation for outbound sending. A button for a high-risk action does not replace checking whether the deliverable is correct.S16, S17, S18, S19, S21, S26

8. Assessments Ready for Competitive Comparison

  1. Strong breadth: Documents/data/design/app development, team projects, connectors, and Chinese office services in one product make it a more complete workbench than “universal Q&A.”
  2. Context is already productized: Project-asset RAG, personal memory, task handoffs, references, shared libraries, and in-place editing all exist. Competitors cannot establish a new category merely by promising to avoid copy-and-paste.
  3. Uneven hardware-integration maturity: AU05-X and LARK A2 control mappings and Plaud MCP have actionable documentation; some of the nine co-branded devices have reached only display/collaboration-announcement status. Label these separately as “documented and operable,” “vendor says live,” “reporter demonstrated a short chain,” or “awaiting version/unknown.”
  4. Clear usage friction: Startup/long-conversation/long-task stability, model/tool-configuration boundaries, and orchestration delays for small tasks are more testable pain points than the abstract claim that “AI is not smart enough.”
  5. Insufficient naturalistic validation: Public material is dominated by launch-period media, training, and tip promotion, with little longitudinal evidence from ordinary business users. This is a research limitation, not grounds to infer either “nobody uses it” or “it must be reliable.”

10. Checklist for Hands-On Verification

The full-product review of public evidence is complete, but “comprehensive research” must not masquerade as hands-on testing that was never done. Priority gaps: ① the current personal edition's local/cloud feature matrix; ② concurrent human/AI editing and undo in the same complex Office file; ③ 8-hour tasks, screen lock, and reconnection recovery; ④ exact SKUs, firmware, regions, inventory, and subscription dependencies for all nine devices; ⑤ replayable tasks using real materials from ordinary HR/finance/sales users. Each test should retain version, model, original input, execution record, output, elapsed time, and human corrections to support fair cross-product comparisons.

Official claims, third-party testing and individual feedback are labeled separately. Individual reports do not establish overall satisfaction. Unknown and unverified points remain explicit evidence gaps.
